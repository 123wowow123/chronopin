## Research and eligibility

Researched official parks, wildlife refuges, forest agencies, county recreation pages, tourism boards and destination-specific reporting. Town destinations include multiple published attractions, outdoor areas or heritage activities; they are regional planning anchors rather than a single attraction’s entrance. Matched geocodes to named destinations and used published California/Texas/Virginia coordinates where available. Final counts were recomputed after entrance-coordinate corrections and day-only classifications. The 250 km measure is straight-line distance; driving, ferry and access time may be longer.

Retained flexible one-day and overnight editorial plans, with lodging, tours, permits, equipment and reservations arranged separately. These are not operator packages or guaranteed two-day admission. Annual seasonal windows are not opening dates or daily access guarantees. Research checked on 2026-10-26.

Excluded unopened Powderhorn, Kronkosky and Post Oak Ridge parks; closed Pine Valley Recreation Area; insufficient Crawford Notch and Fort Morgan activity evidence; and generic or unrelated sources. Removed a statewide Arizona mountain-bike directory mistakenly matched by the geocoder to a local trail: its newly seeded pin was soft-deleted, not offered as a destination. Added Mount San Jacinto, Salton Sea and Ocotillo Wells using official destination pages. Generic municipal homepages were replaced with actual visitor information or excluded. Oracle State Park and San Francisco’s Oracle Park were distinguished by location rather than name alone.

The Kentucky park site sends content inside scripts before rendering; opened the actual pages in a browser and captured their rendered content, rather than accepting an empty shell or search snippet as evidence. Full captures, coordinate matches, excluded candidates and failed requests remain in ignored .scrape/activity-trips-50/. Source evidence is synchronized in scripts/activities/tripResearch.json.

## Photography

All 311 new destinations have visually reviewed photography with source pages, image URLs, captions, dimensions, credits and SHA-256 hashes in src/server/data/activityPhotos.json. Files are content-addressed WebP blobs under Azure thumb/activity-images/, verified by fetching public bytes and comparing the checksum. No pin photo binaries were added to Git.

Checked official galleries first; rejected logos, maps, unrelated news photos, event posters, brochure covers and generic background textures. Recovered unstyled Tennessee originals when image derivatives failed; used genuine Georgia park panoramas without upscaling. Where official downloads failed or supplied no suitable photography, searched alternate galleries and image search, including tourism publications, KERA, 5280, Expedia, Wikimedia Commons and local destination galleries. Leadville’s final image is credited to Diane Lange through 5280; Waxahachie to Diane Bayes/Texas Time Travel; Rome bridge to Ashley Phillips; Cartersville uses a public-domain Wikimedia photo. No new photo gaps remain.
